Terrain intelligence for wooded land

A camera sees treetops.
Lidar sees the ground.

On wooded land, that gap is worth millions. Trees hide the hillside, the wet ground, and the buildable pad underneath — and a surface built from photographs counts the canopy as dirt. These four Massachusetts studies measure the ground itself, from public data anyone can re-run.

How it works

Start broad. Fly only when the decision needs it.

Public data can rule out a weak parcel for almost nothing. A fresh flight is worth paying for only after a site has survived that first cut. The sequence exists to spend money in that order.

01 · Screen

Shortlist from public data

Public lidar, parcel records, mapped wetlands, streams, and slope — enough to say which properties deserve a closer look, and which are already out.

02 · Measure

Fly only what survived

Where the public elevation data is too old to trust, current lidar and imagery get collected under an FAA Part 107 flight plan with site access and ground control.

03 · Decide

Turn terrain into choices

Pad locations, earthwork, access grades, drainage, and clearing compared side by side — plus the field, survey, and engineering work the decision still needs next.

Clear professional boundaries

Useful early — not a substitute for final design

What this is for

Planning, comparison, due diligence, scoping, and deciding where field and professional work should focus next.

What this is not

A boundary survey, wetland delineation, geotechnical investigation, construction quantity, bid, drainage design, or stamped engineering product.

Who does the rest

Fresh flights run with an FAA Part 107 pilot. Survey control, authoritative boundaries, civil design, and stamped deliverables come from licensed partners when a project needs them.
