Projects 01–03 each studied one site in depth. This one asks the question that comes first: which sites are even worth studying? I took a 10 km stretch of the I-495 belt, pulled every tax parcel within 1.5 km of the highway across five towns, and ran the same terrain screens on everything vacant and 10+ acres. The shortlist turns out to be short.

The three viable parcels tell three different stories. Pine Island Road is the obvious one — 110 acres, room to move the pad around, and the lowest dirt cost. The 16.5-acre Lumber Street parcel is small but efficient. The Holliston parcel fits a pad but pays for it in grading. And the twelve brown rows aren't worthless — they're just not warehouse sites; several might take a smaller building or stay as mitigation land.

How the screen works

The numbers are checkable

  1. Parcels — MassGIS L3 tax parcels joined to assessor records for Hopkinton, Milford, Holliston, Hopedale and Upton; kept parcels ≥10 acres, classified vacant / forest / developable land, with under $50k of building value, within 1.5 km of the MassDOT I-495 centerline.
  2. Terrain — the state's 2021 bare-earth lidar DEM, resampled to 1 m. On Projects 01–03 my own point-cloud models matched this surface to 2.0–7.6 cm RMSE, which is why it's trustworthy enough for screening. Wetlands and streams: DEP layers with 100 ft / 200 ft buffers.
  3. The pad search — the same optimizer from Project 03, run inside every parcel: every possible pad position tested, largest pad kept where at least 70% of the window is buildable and 95% sits inside the parcel.
  4. What screening doesn't do — no canopy metrics, no wetness-discrepancy or rock screens, no access routes, no zoning. Those belong to the per-parcel study (that's Projects 01–03, run on whichever of these parcels matters to you). Costs use the same $20/yd³ basis with the same caveats as the other projects.

Screening-level, from public data. A shortlist this small means the full study is affordable for every parcel on it. Also run per project: terrain-wetness discrepancy, vernal-pool candidates, surface roughness. Scripts and pipeline files available on request.
